# ENAE-410---Design-Project
Conceptualize, design, manufacture and test a small RC aircraft.
Project Overview

Students will be tasked with designing a small RC aircraft from a provided kit. The project involves several stages, each of which is critical to the successful completion of the final aircraft. The project will be carried out over the duration of the term and will culminate in a test flight and the submission of a final project report.

**The aircraft has the following requirements:**
• Must weigh less than 250 grams (249.9 g would be acceptable, 250.1 g would not).
• The aircraft may be hand-launched, launched using a launch rail, or can roll on landing gear.
• The aircraft must fly and complete one circuit of a soccer field (TBD which field).
– This must be true fixed-wing flight (helicoptering at a high pitch angle is not acceptable).
• The aircraft must be primarily 3D printed (in LW-PLA or PLA).
• The aircraft electronic and motor components must be those provided by the class (no outside motors, etc.).

**Project Stages**
1. Conceptual Design: Determine the sizing and overall performance parameters of the RC aircraft based on the components available in the provided kit. This involves a comprehensive understanding of aerodynamics and material considerations.
2. Detailed Design and Analysis: Based on the conceptual design, students will perform a detailed design and analysis of the RC aircraft. This will involve simulations and mathematical modeling to validate the conceptual design.
3. Manufacturing: Using the school’s 3D printers and other tools, students will manufacture their RC aircraft. They will need to ensure their design can be manufactured within the given resources and constraints.
4. Test Flight: Towards the end of the semester, students will complete a test flight of the RC aircraft. They should document the performance of the aircraft, any problems encountered, and any necessary adjustments.
5. Final Report: Students will compile their designs into a final report, detailing the process they followed, the decisions they made, the challenges they encountered, and the performance of their aircraft.
